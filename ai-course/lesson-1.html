1. AI at Work. Welcome to AI at Work. In the next minute, you will see how to use generative AI productively and responsibly in a professional environment.

2. What is generative AI? Generative AI can help draft text, suggest ideas, summarise material, or structure information. It predicts useful responses, but it does not guarantee factual accuracy.

3. AI is an assistant. A good use case is preparing a first draft of a meeting agenda, a training outline, or a message using information you are allowed to share.

4. Good inputs matter. Instead of saying write an email, explain the audience, purpose, tone, and required format. Clear instructions improve the usefulness of the draft.

5. Pause before you paste. Do not enter confidential client information, personal records, or restricted documents into unapproved AI tools. Follow your organisation’s data and technology policies.

6. Check before you share. A polished answer may still contain errors, invented references, or unsuitable advice. Check meaningful claims against reliable sources and keep human approval in the workflow.

7. The human stays accountable. Use the model to support judgment, not replace it. Be especially careful with legal, financial, or other consequential decisions. Escalate uncertain cases.

8. Ready to practise? Now continue the course. Decide when to use AI, practise a stronger prompt, and apply responsible review to a realistic workplace scenario.

What is generative AI?

Generative AI creates new content such as text, images, audio or code from patterns learned during training. You give the system an instruction (a prompt); it generates a response based on that instruction and the context it can access.

THE CORE IDEA

Generative AI is helpful at producing a first draft. It does not automatically know whether that draft is true, current, authorised or appropriate.

⌕

Search and retrieval

Finds or retrieves information from available sources. It may help you inspect original material, though search results still require evaluation.

✦

Generative AI

Produces a new response: a summary, draft, table or suggested plan. Even fluent answers may contain invented information.

Three things to remember

01

Fluency is not evidence

A confident tone, citation-looking text or precise number can still be wrong. Verify significant claims against dependable source material.

02

Context changes quality

The more clearly you explain the task, audience, constraints and approved sources, the more useful the first draft may be.

03

People remain accountable

An employee, not the model, must judge whether output is accurate, safe and fit for its intended audience.

WORKPLACE EXAMPLE

Prepare a meeting agenda

A team lead asks an approved AI tool to suggest an agenda for a non-confidential team planning meeting. It drafts a practical structure in seconds. The lead still checks priorities, names and timing before sending it.
