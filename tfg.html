01 / THE CAPABILITY CHALLENGE

Build leaders who can act, not just recall frameworks.

Leadership development spans different career transitions. A first-time manager, an experienced people leader and a senior successor are unlikely to need identical practice, feedback or performance evidence.

DOCUMENTED EXPERIENCE

What the role involved

  • Directing end-to-end design, delivery and management of an accelerated leadership programme.
  • Supporting structured successor development journeys from new management through executive leadership.
  • Translating enterprise capability requirements into scalable, blended learning frameworks.
  • Coaching fellow designers, providing quality assurance and collaborating with leaders and subject-matter experts.
DESIGN THESIS · ILLUSTRATIVE ANALYSIS

Start with the leadership moments that matter.

The most useful starting question is not “Which modules should we build?” It is “What must leaders do differently when the decision is difficult?” The pathway should connect required behaviours, credible rehearsal and timely feedback.

That creates a route from capability expectations to workplace application, rather than a calendar of learning events.

02 / HOW THE DESIGN CAN BE STRUCTURED

From capability expectations to on-the-job behaviour.

An illustrative four-stage design logic for an accelerated leadership pathway. Each stage produces something concrete that can guide learning design and review.

01

Define the role transition

Consult business leaders and SMEs to identify pivotal decisions, behavioural expectations and the capability gap at each leadership level.

02

Align outcomes and evidence

Write observable objectives: diagnose a team issue, hold a coaching conversation, justify a trade-off or propose an action plan.

03

Design repeated practice

Combine short conceptual inputs, authentic decision scenarios, peer dialogue, reflection and manager-supported application.

04

Review transfer and readiness

Use scenario responses, workplace examples and coaching observations to improve the pathway and inform readiness discussions.

Capability need→Observable behaviour→Guided practice→Workplace evidence
03 / INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN RATIONALE

Three decisions that make leadership learning more useful.

These proposed choices demonstrate why an approach could work—not a claim that a specific sequence was delivered at TFG.

DECISION A · AUDIENCE

Differentiate by leadership transition

Why: Equal content for everyone does not mean equal relevance. New managers may need coaching and delegation practice, while senior leaders need to weigh wider trade-offs and ambiguity.

Learning principle: Relevance and constructive alignment—match performance expectations and practice to the role.

DECISION B · PRACTICE

Replace passive explanation with consequential choices

Why: Knowing a leadership model is not the same as applying it in a difficult conversation. Branching prompts, realistic cases and specific feedback can surface the judgement that needs strengthening.

Learning principle: Authentic assessment, retrieval and feedback before high-stakes application.

DECISION C · SCALE

Build a repeatable design and coaching standard

Why: A large enterprise needs consistency without losing context. Shared storyboards, review criteria and facilitator guidance support quality across designers and cohorts.

Learning principle: Quality assurance, cognitive clarity and reinforcement in the work environment.

A team is missing targets, but the cause is unclear.

DESIGN ALIGNMENT

From task to evidence

Learning objective

Diagnose a people-performance situation and propose a fair first action.

Learner activity

Select a response, explain the rationale and revise an action plan after feedback.

Feedback and assessment

Compare against a simple decision rubric: evidence, fairness, communication and follow-through.

05 / EVIDENCE OF EFFECTIVENESS

Measure capability and transfer—not only attendance.

A proposed evaluation plan, moving from learning to behaviour. There are no numerical programme results claimed in this case study.

During learning

Assess decisions in realistic scenarios and track rubric-based improvement after feedback.

During application

Use voluntary, appropriately approved manager observations and learner action plans to understand transfer.

At programme review

Review readiness indicators with stakeholders, compare against a defined baseline and refine the pathway.

Evidence standard: Documented responsibilities are based on the CV. Participation, promotion, performance or ROI figures have not been invented; programme-specific results would require approved source evidence.
