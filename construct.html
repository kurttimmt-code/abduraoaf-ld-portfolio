01 / THE LEARNING CHALLENGE

Design for application, not just content coverage.

Complex postgraduate subject matter must become something learners can question, compare, practise and defend. Online delivery also requires clarity about the next action and feedback when an instructor is not immediately present.

DOCUMENTED EXPERIENCE

Team-based digital learning design

  • Collaborating with a specialist instructional design team developing evidence-based online courses for the University of Tennessee MBA programme.
  • Applying learning-science principles to complex academic and business concepts.
  • Supporting engaging digital and blended postgraduate programmes.
  • Managing concurrent learning projects to meet client expectations and delivery requirements.
DESIGN THESIS · ILLUSTRATIVE ANALYSIS

Every digital activity should earn its place.

An interaction is useful when it makes a learner interpret evidence, practise a decision or receive feedback. A polished screen alone is not proof of learning.

The design question becomes: which learner action best demonstrates the intended intellectual skill?

03 / INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN RATIONALE

Why the learning mechanics matter.

These choices articulate an instructional-design approach appropriate to a demanding digital postgraduate audience, rather than claiming details of a specific client module.

DECISION A · ALIGNMENT

Begin with assessable thinking

Why: A goal such as “understand strategic options” is hard to assess. “Compare options and justify a recommendation with evidence” gives the designer and learner a clear target.

Learning principle: Constructive alignment and higher-order cognitive objectives.

DECISION B · ENGAGEMENT

Make complex ideas visible through a case

Why: Realistic trade-offs invite postgraduate learners to apply frameworks, challenge assumptions and explain reasoning rather than simply recognise terminology.

Learning principle: Problem-centred adult learning, worked examples and guided practice.

DECISION C · INCLUSION

Remove avoidable digital friction

Why: A learner should not need to overcome a confusing interface to demonstrate domain knowledge. Plain instructions, accessible controls, readable structure and flexible response formats support participation.

Learning principle: Universal Design for Learning and accessibility-minded quality assurance.

04 / ILLUSTRATIVE STORYBOARD

A digital MBA decision case, mapped to its learning goal.

This independent example shows how a storyboard connects learner decisions, feedback and assessment. It is not derived from confidential curriculum material.

FICTIONAL MBA-STYLE LEARNING ACTIVITY

Which market-entry option is most defensible?

A team is weighing three growth options with incomplete financial and customer evidence. Learners compare the alternatives, state assumptions, select a recommendation and anticipate one major risk.

STORYBOARD LOGIC

What the learner does—and why

Objective

Evaluate strategic options using evidence and clearly articulated assumptions.

Interaction

Inspect a short data brief, record a choice and explain the trade-off before seeing expert feedback.

Assessment

Use an explicit rubric for evidence, reasoning, assumptions and communication—not only the chosen option.

05 / QUALITY AND LEARNING EVIDENCE

Evaluate the design as well as the learner outcome.

For digital learning, success requires strong alignment and an experience that learners can navigate and use. These measures illustrate an evaluation approach, not verified client results.

Quality of reasoning

Review application against a transparent rubric; compare examples before and after feedback where appropriate.

Learner experience

Observe points of confusion, task completion, clarity of instructions and usefulness of feedback.

Delivery quality

Check navigation, captions, keyboard access, content accuracy and alignment with approved academic requirements.
