01 / DESIGN RATIONALE

Instructional methods matched to performance requirements.

Adult learning / relevance

Start from real work

Practice focuses on drafting, document reviews and client communication rather than abstract AI terminology.

Scaffolding / cognitive load

Build confidence in steps

Short lessons move from explanation and modelling to guided practice and independent decisions.

Multimedia learning

Use media with purpose

Introductory video connects a voice-led explanation with concise visual cues; captions and a transcript are provided.

Retrieval / feedback

Make misconceptions visible

Knowledge checks and scenarios provide immediate feedback and opportunities to retry decisions.

Learning transfer

Provide job aids

The V.A.L.I.D. checklist and prompt template can support workplace decisions after the course ends.

Universal access

Design for more learners

Readable content, keyboard-operable activities and text alternatives are goals; formal WCAG 2.2 AA validation is still required.

02 / ASSESSMENT STRATEGY

Check decisions, not just definitions.

Embedded formative practice

Lesson checks identify misunderstandings while learners are still practising. The interactive prompt builder produces a usable draft, and the client-update scenario requires three sequential judgments.

  • Non-punitive retry opportunities
  • Explanatory feedback, not just right/wrong labels
  • Applied, fictional tasks that avoid real client information

Final mastery check

Ten applied questions assess safe, useful and accountable AI use. The current course uses an 80% pass mark and shows the correct reasoning after submission.

  • Pass threshold: 8 of 10 questions
  • Immediate question-level explanations
  • Results retained on the learner's device, not a central LMS

A pass score indicates performance on this short knowledge assessment; it does not independently prove workplace behaviour change.

03 / EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

Four levels of evidence, from relevance to results.

LEVEL 1

Reaction

Was learning relevant, clear and useful? Gather immediate learner feedback on practical confidence and ease of use.

LEVEL 2

Learning

Use question scores, scenario choices and reviewed prompt samples to test stated objectives.

LEVEL 3

Behaviour

After 30–60 days, review approved AI-use practices, work samples and manager observations, subject to consent and policy.

LEVEL 4

Results

Where baseline and attribution allow, analyse accuracy, rework, turnaround time and safe adoption quality.

Kirkpatrick-inspired evaluation plan. Levels 3 and 4 are proposed future measurements, not available data from this demonstration.

04 / PILOT & MEASUREMENT

What would prove the course works?

CompletionEngagement signal, not the definition of impact
AssessmentItem-level mastery and common misconceptions
Prompt qualityFour-criterion practical rubric on anonymised samples
BehaviourAppropriate tool approval, validation and sign-off practices

A pragmatic pilot sequence

  1. Validate audience needs and real workplace constraints.
  2. Establish a baseline for confidence and safe-use practices.
  3. Run a small accessible pilot and collect learning evidence.
  4. Interview stakeholders; refine confusing interactions and content.
  5. Define scale criteria only after results and governance review.

Implementation and quality assurance

  • Validate audience needs and test the learning experience with a representative pilot before organisational use.
  • Completion and assessment progress are stored only on the device.
  • Prompt-builder output is not automatically scored.
  • Screen reader, keyboard and WCAG conformance require formal testing.
  • No verified productivity, adoption or organisational ROI data.

Learning success is behavioural.

The course demonstrates an instructional approach. A real deployment would test whether professionals use AI more effectively and more responsibly in their day-to-day work.
