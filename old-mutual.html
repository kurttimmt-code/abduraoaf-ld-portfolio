01 / THE PERFORMANCE CHALLENGE

People need clarity at the moment they make a decision.

Onboarding and process change create a familiar gap: employees may understand a communication but still be unsure what action is expected when real work becomes complex or exceptional.

PROFESSIONAL CONTRIBUTION

Consulting, design and evaluation

  • Designing and executing targeted onboarding and change-learning solutions for Old Mutual Finance.
  • Consulting cross-functional stakeholders about current and future capability requirements.
  • Building learning experiences aligned with business outcomes and workplace transfer.
  • Evaluating impact, adoption and ROI through established models and post-training metrics.
INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN PERSPECTIVE

Teach the decision, not just the policy text.

A sound design identifies the tasks and judgement points where uncertainty matters most. Learners need the correct action, the reason it matters, the source to consult and when to escalate.

That makes performance support and assessment central to change enablement.

02 / CONSULTING-TO-DESIGN APPROACH

Map the critical task before selecting the format.

A task-centred approach to designing change and onboarding interventions with workplace adoption in mind.

01

Discover the friction

Ask stakeholders which task is changing, where people hesitate, what errors matter and how success would be observed.

02

Specify safe decisions

Separate mandatory knowledge from actions, safeguards, documentation needs and escalation moments.

03

Practise and support

Use concise examples, judgement scenarios, immediate feedback and a reference aid available at the point of work.

04

Check adoption

Review knowledge checks, process confidence and verified workplace signals to identify where support remains necessary.

Change in task→Decision point→Guided response→Quality & adoption
03 / INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN RATIONALE

Choices that reduce risk and support application.

Three instructional design choices suited to a financial-services learning challenge.

DECISION A · RELEVANCE

Organise around tasks, not an information dump

Why: Learners must know what to do, not memorise every sentence of source material. Break a complex process into moments of action, reference and escalation.

Learning principle: Cognitive-load management and performance-centred design.

DECISION B · SAFETY

Use realistic judgement and feedback

Why: A simple recall quiz can miss errors in selecting a safe course of action. Scenarios can assess verification, documentation and appropriate escalation without exposing real customer data.

Learning principle: Situated practice and formative feedback.

DECISION C · CHANGE

Make the correct behaviour easy to repeat

Why: New behaviour needs reinforcement after formal training. A brief checklist and clearly signposted source of truth can help employees when a rare case arises.

Learning principle: Spaced reinforcement and learning transfer.

04 / WORKED DESIGN EXAMPLE

A safe-decision activity for onboarding.

A task simulation that lets learners practise safe decisions without using client information.

CLIENT-SERVICE PRACTICE ACTIVITY

There is a request, but the information is incomplete.

A customer-facing employee receives a request to change account-related information, but a required detail is unclear. The activity asks what should be verified, whether work should proceed and how to find the approved escalation route.

LEARNING ALIGNMENT

Practice before real consequences

Learning objective

Recognise uncertainty and choose a safe next step using approved guidance.

Learner activity

Select from three possible responses and explain the control or information gap behind the choice.

Feedback and support

Explain the decision principle, indicate where official procedures would be consulted and reinforce escalation rather than guessing.

05 / EVALUATION AND CHANGE ADOPTION

Look for accurate decisions and sustained adoption.

A performance-oriented evaluation approach connects knowledge, safe decisions and sustained workplace adoption.

Decision quality

Scenario accuracy, confidence calibration and correct identification of escalation conditions.

Time to readiness

Role-specific onboarding milestones, supported practice and manager confirmation of task readiness.

Quality of adoption

Approved process checks, observable behaviour and follow-up feedback, interpreted against a baseline.
